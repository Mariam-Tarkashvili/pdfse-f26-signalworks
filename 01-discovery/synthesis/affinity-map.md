# Affinity Map

Pull every quote from every real log into a cluster below. A cluster counts as a pattern only when it holds quotes from 2 or more different interviews. Draft from logs 01-06; teammates add quotes as new logs land.

## Pattern 1

**Pattern:** Students cannot tell how long the cafeteria wait will take before they commit to the line.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02, 03

**Quotes:**
- "You never know in advance whether you'll need 5 minutes or 25." (01)
- "I usually text a friend - 'Is the line big?' - and then go down." (02)
- "I never know when there are many people, you can't determine it in advance, it's at different times every day." (03)

## Pattern 2

**Pattern:** When the line looks too long or too risky, students leave the cafeteria for Spar, delivery, or skip lunch entirely.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02, 03

**Quotes:**
- "When I see the line, I don't even enter, I just buy a sandwich at Spar." (01)
- "During exam week I don't eat lunch at all, I sit hungry until evening." (02)
- "I'd rather take a quiz hungry than stand in that queue." (02)
- "If the line is big, I just go up to my room and order food delivery." (03)

## Pattern 3

**Pattern:** Standing in the queue costs class time or wasted minutes that still end with no meal.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 03

**Quotes:**
- "Twice I was 15 minutes late to class because the line was moving so slowly." (01)
- "Once I stood for 20 minutes and in the end they ran out of food entirely." (03)

## Pattern 4

**Pattern:** The cues students use to judge the line from outside (a look from the door, a message to a friend) do not tell them how fast it will move.

**Interviews behind it:** 04, 05

**Quotes:**
- "Even yesterday I skipped lunch - I got to the door, took a look at the line, and since I had no idea how long I'd have to wait, I went straight to the library." (04)
- "The worst part is that the line looks small from the outside, but inside it gets stuck at the register and you don't realize it until you're all the way in." (05)
- "I usually text a friend who is at the canteen 'is it crowded?' and even they reply 'I don't know, it's moving slowly for some reason'. You still can't figure out if it's worth going down." (05)

## Pattern 5

**Pattern:** The same line can clear in a couple of minutes one day and stall for 20 or more the next, so students cannot plan a break around it.

**Interviews behind it:** 04, 05, 06

**Quotes:**
- "When I go down during break, people are standing in line and you genuinely have no way of knowing if it'll take 5 minutes or 20 minutes." (04)
- "When you go down from the library, it's a gamble - you might be out in 2 minutes, or you might stand there for 25 minutes and be late to your group meeting." (05)
- "A few times I went in, stood for 5 minutes, the line didn't move at all, and I was forced to leave without food just to not be late for lecture." (06)

## Pattern 6

**Pattern:** Students with fixed time blocks stop risking the canteen at peak hours and choose a guaranteed alternative (a skipped meal or food brought from home) over possibly being late.

**Interviews behind it:** 04, 06

**Quotes:**
- "I prefer staying hungry over being late to a lecture, because you can never predict in advance whether the line will move fast or get stuck." (04)
- "I have exactly 30 minutes free. If the line took 10 minutes it would be fine, but because it's impossible to calculate the time in advance, I can't take the risk." (06)
- "I don't even try going down during peak hours anymore - I bring sandwiches from home, solely to avoid the line uncertainty." (06)
