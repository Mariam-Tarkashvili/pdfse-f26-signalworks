# Affinity Map

Pull every quote from every real log into a cluster below. A cluster counts as a pattern only when it holds quotes from 2 or more different interviews. Built from logs 01-10: 30 quotes, 3 per log, each used once.

> **Note:** logs 07-10 are sample logs (invented). Swap their quotes for real ones as those interviews happen, then re-check every cluster that leans on them.

## Pattern 1

**Pattern:** Standing at the canteen door, students cannot tell whether the wait will be 5 minutes or 25.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 03, 04, 05, 07

**Quotes:**
- "You never know in advance whether you'll need 5 minutes or 25." (01)
- "I never know when there are many people, you can't determine it in advance, it's at different times every day." (03)
- "When I go down during break, people are standing in line and you genuinely have no way of knowing if it'll take 5 minutes or 20 minutes." (04)
- "When you go down from the library, it's a gamble - you might be out in 2 minutes, or you might stand there for 25 minutes and be late to your group meeting." (05)
- "I stand at the door and can't tell whether it's five minutes or twenty-five." (07)

## Pattern 2

**Pattern:** The checks students run before committing (a text to a friend, a look from the door or a window) show how many people are there, not how fast the line will move.

**Interviews behind it:** 02, 05, 07, 10

**Quotes:**
- "I usually text a friend - 'Is the line big?' - and then go down." (02)
- "The worst part is that the line looks small from the outside, but inside it gets stuck at the register and you don't realize it until you're all the way in." (05)
- "I usually text a friend who is at the canteen 'is it crowded?' and even they reply 'I don't know, it's moving slowly for some reason'. You still can't figure out if it's worth going down." (05)
- "I text a groupmate: how's the line? Sometimes she answers, sometimes not." (07)
- "I look from the second-floor window. If people are standing all the way to the door, I don't go down." (10)

## Pattern 3

**Pattern:** Faced with the line at the door, students walk away without joining it: to Spar, to delivery, or to no lunch at all.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 02, 03, 04

**Quotes:**
- "When I see the line, I don't even enter, I just buy a sandwich at Spar." (01)
- "I'd rather take a quiz hungry than stand in that queue." (02)
- "If the line is big, I just go up to my room and order food delivery." (03)
- "Even yesterday I skipped lunch - I got to the door, took a look at the line, and since I had no idea how long I'd have to wait, I went straight to the library." (04)

## Pattern 4

**Pattern:** Students join the line, spend a large part of their break in it, and then abandon it with no food because the next class is starting.

**Interviews behind it:** 06, 07

**Quotes:**
- "A few times I went in, stood for 5 minutes, the line didn't move at all, and I was forced to leave without food just to not be late for lecture." (06)
- "On Tuesday I joined, waited fifteen minutes and left anyway, my lecture was starting." (07)

## Pattern 5

**Pattern:** Students wait through the whole line and the food is gone by the time their turn comes.

**Interviews behind it:** 03, 08

**Quotes:**
- "Once I stood for 20 minutes and in the end they ran out of food entirely." (03)
- "I stood for twenty minutes, and when my turn came there was no hot food left." (08)
- "If I had known the food was running out, I would not have joined the line at all." (08)

## Pattern 6

**Pattern:** Staying in the line makes students late to class, and the lateness is punished: missed lecture time or a refused attendance mark.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 08

**Quotes:**
- "Twice I was 15 minutes late to class because the line was moving so slowly." (01)
- "I was seven minutes late to the lecture and the lecturer did not let me sign the attendance sheet." (08)

## Pattern 7

**Pattern:** Students with a fixed break have stopped gambling on the canteen and take a guaranteed option instead: food from home or going hungry.

**Interviews behind it:** 04, 06, 09

**Quotes:**
- "I prefer staying hungry over being late to a lecture, because you can never predict in advance whether the line will move fast or get stuck." (04)
- "I have exactly 30 minutes free. If the line took 10 minutes it would be fine, but because it's impossible to calculate the time in advance, I can't take the risk." (06)
- "I don't even try going down during peak hours anymore - I bring sandwiches from home, solely to avoid the line uncertainty." (06)
- "I bring a container from home and eat in the corridor." (09)

## Pattern 8

**Pattern:** In exam weeks the routines that work on a normal day break down, and students stop eating lunch.

**Interviews behind it:** 02, 10

**Quotes:**
- "During exam week I don't eat lunch at all, I sit hungry until evening." (02)
- "In exam week that stops working. Everyone comes out at once and you cannot predict anything." (10)

## Not yet a pattern

Single-interview quotes. Move one up when a second interview echoes it.

- **The break is too short to eat even with no line.** Only interview 09 so far. This is Beka's belief, and it cuts against our hypothesis: for her the line is not the problem.
  - "I have fifteen minutes. I don't even think about the canteen." (09)
  - "Even if there were no line at all, I still would not have time to sit down and eat." (09)
- **A regular has learned when the line is short.** Only interview 10 so far. It contradicts interview 03 ("it's at different times every day").
  - "On a normal day I know: at one o'clock it is full, by half past one it is already empty." (10)
