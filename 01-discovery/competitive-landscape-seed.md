# Competitive Landscape Seed

Every workaround and alternative our interviewees told us they use today, with the log it came from. Nothing here was found by searching online. No interviewee mentioned any existing tool or service for checking the canteen line.

## Ways they try to find out before committing

| What they do | Logs | Where it falls short, in their words |
|---|---|---|
| Look at the line from the entrance or glass door and count heads (turn around if it passes the doorway, or if there are more than 5 or 6 people) | 01, 04 | Shows how many people, not how fast it moves: it "looks small from the outside" and is stuck at the register (05) |
| Walk in and judge it from inside | 03 | Costs the walk and the minutes; once 20 minutes and no food (03) |
| Look down from the second-floor window before walking over | 10 | Same head count, from further away |
| Message a friend who is already there (Messenger, Instagram, Telegram) | 02, 05, 07 | The friend does not always answer (07), or answers "I don't know, it's moving slowly for some reason" (05) |
| Ask people walking out with trays what food is left | 08 | Only works once you are already at the door |
| Join the line and leave if it does not move | 06, 07 | Five to fifteen minutes of the break gone, and no meal (06, 07) |

## Ways they eat without the canteen line

| What they do | Logs | What it costs them |
|---|---|---|
| Snacks from Spar, the campus shop or the shop outside the gate | 01, 07, 08, 10 | 4 to 6 GEL, and it is not a meal |
| Snickers or coffee from the hallway vending machine | 04 | Poor concentration by the 15:00 seminar |
| Go back to the dorm for delivery or instant noodles | 03 | 8 to 10 GEL extra on delivery |
| Order Glovo or Wolt to campus about 45 minutes ahead | 06 | 8 to 10 GEL extra per order, and it has to be planned before the break |
| Bring food from home (sandwiches, a lunchbox) and eat in the corridor | 06, 09 | Cooking the night before, about 20 minutes (09); forgot it once and did not eat from 9:00 to 16:00 (09) |

## Ways they change the timing

| What they do | Logs | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|
| Wait out the first 20 minutes of the break and go when the rush has passed | 10 | Needs a break of 40 minutes or more, and stops working in exam weeks (10) |
| Put lunch off until classes finish, around 15:30 to 16:00 | 05 | Three hours late; the food may be gone by then (after 14:00 in 08) |

## Doing nothing

| What they do | Logs | What it costs them |
|---|---|---|
| Skip lunch | 02, 04, 07, 10 | Hungry until evening (02), until 17:00 (07); two skipped lunches a week (04) |

## What this tells us

- The most common workaround is the head count from the door (01, 04, 10), and it measures the wrong thing.
- The closest thing to a real information source is a friend inside the line (02, 05, 07), and it fails because even the friend cannot tell how long it will take.
- Everything in the second and third tables is a way of giving up on a canteen meal at the time the student wanted it.

## Not counted

One interviewee asked to be told if a "real-time queue tracker" is ever launched (06). That is a feature request, not something anyone uses today, so it is not in the tables.
